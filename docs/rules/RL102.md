# RL102 `iframe-title`

Frames must have a title describing their content.

## WCAG

- [4.1.2 Name, Role, Value](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/name-role-value.html) (level A)

## Why it matters

Screen readers list frames by their `title` so users can decide whether to enter them. An untitled map, video or payment widget is announced as just "frame".

## What ramplint reports

- `<iframe>` and `<frame>` without a non-empty `title`, `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`.

## Examples

Problems:

```django
{# ✗ #} <iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/{{ video.id }}"></iframe>
```

Fixed:

```django
{# ✓ #} <iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/{{ video.id }}" title="{{ video.title }}"></iframe>
{# ✓ #} <iframe src="{% url 'store:map' %}" title="Store location map"></iframe>
```

## Not reported

- Frames hidden from assistive technology with `aria-hidden="true"` (for example tracking frames).

## Suppressing

```django
{# ramplint: ignore RL102 #}
```

on the line with the problem or the line above it, or for the whole file:

```django
{# ramplint: ignore-file RL102 #}
```

You can also disable the rule in `pyproject.toml`:

```toml
[tool.ramplint]
ignore = ["RL102"]
```
